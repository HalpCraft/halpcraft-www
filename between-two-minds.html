BetweenTwo Minds

Same mind. A different outcome.

Between Two Minds helps you think clearly when something keeps replaying: a conversation that didn’t go the way you wanted, or a decision you keep circling. It’s a short reflection told like a comic: pick the version of you that showed up, name the voice that pushed back, and answer a few honest questions written for that exact match-up.

What it is

Some days the hardest conversation is the one in your own head.

You already know the voice. It tells you to wait, to double-check, to carry it all yourself.

Between Two Minds gives that voice a name and a face, and puts it up against the best version of you. Then it asks you a few honest questions about what actually happened.

It isn’t a personality test and it isn’t homework. It’s a few minutes of clear thinking, dressed as a comic, so it’s easier to be honest with yourself.

How a battle works

Five panels. A few honest minutes.

Every combination of hero, villain and outcome changes the questions, so no two battles read the same.

  1. 01

    Choose your cast

    Pick the hero, the version of you that showed up. Then pick the villain, the voice that pushed back. Any hero can face any villain.

  2. 02

    Who has the wheel?

    Right now, as you see it. There’s no wrong answer. This is where the reflection starts.

    HeroContestedVillain

  3. 03

    Choose your depth

    Pick the space you have today. Deeper rounds add more prompts, not more pressure.

    • Quick, 3 prompts, about 2 to 3 minutes
    • Standard, 5 prompts, about 4 to 6 minutes
    • Deep, 7 prompts, about 7 to 10 minutes
  4. 04

    Tell it straight

    Set the scene in your own words, then work the questions this battle threw up. Finish with one good thing, and what you’ll carry forward.

  5. 05

    Log the battle

    Save it to your story. Over time you’ll see which heroes turn up for you, and which villains keep winning.

The cast

Five heroes. Five villains. All of them you.

Every hero brings two powers. So does every villain, along with a line it likes to use. You’ll recognise a few.

The heroes

  1. Horizon

    Sees what things could be, and gets other people to see it too.

    Powers

    Far sightSees the version of this that does not exist yet.

    PullMakes other people want to go there too.

  2. The Lens

    Cuts through the noise and names what is actually going on.

    Powers

    X-rayFinds the real problem under everything being said about it.

    Plain speechNames it in one sentence anyone can follow.

  3. The Mender

    Drawn to what is broken, in things or between people. Closes the gap.

    Powers

    Gap senseSpots what is broken before anyone says so.

    Quiet repairCloses it without being asked and without fuss.

  4. The Forge

    Steps up when it is heavy. Holds a line nobody else is holding.

    Powers

    Load bearingHolds weight that would stop most people.

    FinisherGets it done after everyone else has drifted off.

  5. The Signal

    Reads people, and says the true thing out loud.

    Powers

    Deep readHears what people are not saying.

    Straight talkSays the true thing while it still matters.

The villains

  1. The Mirage

    Keeps the real version of you somewhere up ahead, so this one never counts.

    Powers

    Moving lineShifts the finish line every time you get near it.

    ShineMakes the next thing look brighter than the one in your hands.

    “You will be the real thing once the next one is done.”

  2. The Fog

    Makes being certain the price of being allowed to speak.

    Powers

    The unknownsFor every answer, three more things you should have checked.

    StallTurns waiting into something that sounds like research.

    “You do not know enough yet. Someone else knows more.”

  3. The Unpicker

    Treats one flaw as the proof. Reopens what was already settled.

    Powers

    Flaw finderFinds the one wrong thing in anything you have done.

    RewindReopens decisions that were already made.

    “They will find the part you got wrong.”

  4. The Furnace

    Turns proving you deserve it into the job. Stopping feels like being caught.

    Powers

    Noble weightMakes carrying everything feel like the right thing to do.

    AlarmMakes stopping feel like you are about to be found out.

    “If you put it down, they will see.”

  5. The Echo

    Keeps you agreeable so nobody looks too closely.

    Powers

    False kindnessMakes going along with it feel like being nice.

    Volume downTurns your voice down until the point is gone.

    “Do not make this harder than it needs to be.”

The villains aren’t other people. They’re the habits capable people carry, at work and at home. Naming one is half the fight.

When to use it

Whenever something keeps replaying.

Between Two Minds works best close to the moment, while it still has a hold on you.

  • After a conversation that didn’t go the way you wanted.
  • Before a decision you keep circling.
  • When something small is taking up a lot of room.
  • At the end of a long week, to put it down.

You can pick your cast straight away. Saving a battle needs a Between Two Minds account (your email and a password), so your story is there the next time you sign in.
