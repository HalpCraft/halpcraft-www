BetweenTwo Minds

Same mind. A different outcome.

Between Two Minds helps you think clearly when something keeps replaying: a conversation that didn’t go the way you wanted, or a decision you keep circling. It’s a short reflection told like a comic: pick the version of you that showed up, name the voice that pushed back, and answer a few honest questions written for that exact match-up.

What it is

Some days the hardest conversation is the one in your own head.

You already know the voice. It tells you to wait, to double-check, to carry it all yourself.

Between Two Minds gives that voice a name and a face, and puts it up against the best version of you. Then it asks you a few honest questions about what actually happened.

It isn’t a personality test and it isn’t homework. It’s a few minutes of clear thinking, dressed as a comic, so it’s easier to be honest with yourself.

How a battle works

Five panels. A few honest minutes.

Every combination of hero, villain and outcome changes the questions, so no two battles read the same.

  1. 01

    Choose your cast

    Pick the hero, the version of you that showed up. Then pick the villain, the voice that pushed back. Any hero can face any villain.

  2. 02

    Who has the wheel?

    Right now, as you see it. There’s no wrong answer. This is where the reflection starts.

    HeroContestedVillain

  3. 03

    Choose your depth

    Pick the space you have today. Deeper rounds add more prompts, not more pressure.

    • Quick, 3 prompts, about 2 to 3 minutes
    • Standard, 5 prompts, about 4 to 6 minutes
    • Deep, 7 prompts, about 7 to 10 minutes
  4. 04

    Tell it straight

    Set the scene in your own words, then work the questions this battle threw up. Finish with one good thing, and what you’ll carry forward.

  5. 05

    Log the battle

    Save it to your story. Over time you’ll see which heroes turn up for you, and which villains keep winning.

The cast

Five heroes. Five villains. All of them you.

Every hero has a strength and a cost. Every villain has a line it likes to use. You’ll recognise a few.

The heroes

  1. Horizon

    Sees what could be and gets others to see it too.

    The costStruggles to stay present long enough to finish.

  2. The Lens

    Cuts through noise and finds the actual problem.

    The costCan want certainty more than progress.

  3. The Mender

    Drawn to what is broken. Closes the gap.

    The costFinds it hard to leave anything alone.

  4. The Forge

    Works under pressure. Holds a standard nobody else holds.

    The costStruggles to let anyone else hold it.

  5. The Signal

    Reads the room and says the true thing.

    The costFeels the cost of friction more than most.

The villains

  1. The Mirage

    Promises the real thing is somewhere else.

    “The real thing is still ahead of this.”

  2. The Fog

    Makes certainty the price of moving.

    “You do not know enough yet.”

  3. The Unpicker

    Will not let settled things stay settled.

    “You missed something. Go back.”

  4. The Furnace

    Turns everything into load, then burns it.

    “If you do not carry it, nobody will.”

  5. The Echo

    Gives the room what it wants.

    “Do not make this harder than it needs to be.”

The villains aren’t other people. They’re the habits every capable leader carries. Naming one is half the fight.

When to use it

Whenever something keeps replaying.

Between Two Minds works best close to the moment, while it still has a hold on you.

  • After a conversation that didn’t go the way you wanted.
  • Before a decision you keep circling.
  • When something small is taking up a lot of room.
  • At the end of a long week, to put it down.

You can pick your cast straight away. Saving a battle needs a Between Two Minds account (your email and a password), so your story is there the next time you sign in.
